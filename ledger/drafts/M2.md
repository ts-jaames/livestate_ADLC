# M2 — 'Lifecycle' reintroduces phase/stage thinking

## Risk
Statement: 'Lifecycle' reintroduces phase/stage thinking. The frame fights the confidence-governed system it sits in.
Category: Usability
Gate: Define
Area: Definition (meta)

## Assumption(s)
### A1
Statement: We believe people who read ADLC materials that use the word "lifecycle" will say the next move is a phase or a calendar step — not "we have to prove this first."
Success criteria: Five people read only the Define page copy and this risk's register row. No glossary. No extra definitions. At least 4 of 5, in one written sentence, say what we do next in terms of proof (what we would have to learn). They do not use phase, stage, sprint, milestone, or timeline on their own.
Failure criteria: 2 or more of 5 describe next work as a phase, stage, or calendar step. Or they cannot say what would have to be proven before we take a hard-to-undo step.

## Slice(s)
### S1 (tests A1)
Method: Five people who already know a traditional SDLC. None of them wrote these drafts. Give them only what a reader would actually see: the Define copy on the client board, and this register row. They each write one sentence: "What determines what we do next?" No talking until all five sentences are in. Two of us score the sentences with a word list the participants never see (phase / stage / sprint / milestone / timeline / schedule / on track vs proof / assumption / slice / signal / confidence / gate).
Type: Usability
Execution: Requires manual run
Scope / time-box: 5 people. 30 minutes. Notes are throwaway.

## Signal
Success signal: 4 or more of 5 sentences talk about proof and use none of the phase/calendar words.
Failure signal: 2 or more of 5 use a phase/calendar word as the reason for next work, or cannot name a proof bar.
Threshold: If it fails, change the wording. Do not treat "lifecycle" as safe. If it passes, we can keep testing other Define risks. Stop only if people cannot do the task at all (the materials are unreadable).
