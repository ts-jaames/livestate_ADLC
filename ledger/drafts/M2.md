# M2 — 'Lifecycle' reintroduces phase/stage thinking

## Risk
Statement: 'Lifecycle' reintroduces phase/stage thinking. The frame fights the confidence-governed system it sits in.
Category: Usability
Gate: Define
Area: Definition (meta)

## Assumption(s)
### A1
Statement: We believe that people who read ADLC materials that use the word "lifecycle" will describe what happens next as a phase, stage, or calendar step rather than as a confidence threshold on a named assumption.
Success criteria: In a timed read-aloud of the current ADLC Define materials (unknown: exact packet — use whatever is in this repo plus the live client board), at least 4 of 5 participants name "what we do next" in confidence/signal terms (assumption, slice, bar, gate) with zero unprompted use of phase/stage/sprint/milestone/timeline language.
Failure criteria: 2 or more of 5 participants, unprompted, describe next work as a phase, stage, or calendar step, or cannot state what would have to be proven before the next irreversible step.

## Slice(s)
### S1 (tests A1)
Method: One sitting: five people who already know a traditional SDLC, none of whom authored these drafts. They read the same short packet (index copy for Define + this risk's register row + glossary terms Confidence / Commitment State only). Each independently writes one sentence: "What determines what we do next?" No discussion until all five sentences are in. Score sentences for phase/calendar vocabulary vs confidence/signal vocabulary against a pre-agreed word list (phase, stage, sprint, milestone, timeline, schedule, on track vs assumption, slice, signal, confidence, gate, promote).
Type: Usability
Execution: Requires manual run
Scope / time-box: n=5; 30 minutes; disposable notes, not a workshop redesign.

## Signal
Success signal: ≥4 of 5 written sentences use confidence/signal terms and contain none of the pre-agreed phase/calendar words.
Failure signal: ≥2 of 5 sentences use a pre-agreed phase/calendar word as the determinant of next work, or cannot name a proof bar.
Threshold: Failure signal → Iterate the frame (wording/materials), do not Promote ADLC-as-lifecycle language. Success signal → framing of "lifecycle" is usable enough to keep testing other Define risks. Stop only if participants cannot complete the task at all (materials unreadable).
