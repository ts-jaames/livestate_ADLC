# RC5 — Confidence-gating requires judgment calls teams aren't equipped to make

## Risk
Statement: Confidence-gating requires judgment calls teams aren't equipped to make. The discipline collapses into guesswork or gets skipped entirely.
Category: Usability
Gate: Define
Area: Roles & Comms

## Assumption(s)
### A1
Statement: We believe three people, with only the written promote / iterate / pivot / stop rules (no class), can look at one finished draft with a filled-in result and pick the same decision as a sealed answer key — and will not skip the decision line.
Success criteria: All 3 match the key. All 3 fill in a decision. None leave it blank or write "looks good" / "ship it" without naming a gate.
Failure criteria: Any one of the three disagrees with the key, leaves the decision blank, or chooses promote because of time pressure or enthusiasm (those are not proof).

## Slice(s)
### S1 (tests A1)
Method: One sample file in the same shape as a ledger draft, with a made-up result written in. One person writes a sealed answer key and is not a judge. Three other people get only the glossary pages on when to move from idea to test, and from test to commit. They mark a decision and which of those moves they think they are in. Compare to the key. Do not use a live client decision as the sample.
Type: Usability
Execution: Requires manual run
Scope / time-box: 3 judges plus 1 key author. 20 minutes. Throw the sample away after scoring unless reviewers keep it as a teaching file.

## Signal
Success signal: 3 of 3 match the key. No skips. No promote-because-we-are-excited.
Failure signal: Any mismatch, skip, or promote from enthusiasm or time.
Threshold: If it fails, change who decides, or give a thinner aid, before we require this judgment in delivery. If it passes, people can apply the written rules on paper. That is not proof they will pause under commercial pressure. Do not mix that into this test.
