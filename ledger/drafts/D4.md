# D4 — Discovery scope is unbounded (tries to define the whole system)

## Risk
Statement: Discovery scope is unbounded (tries to define the whole system). Over-definition — the classic traditional-discovery failure.
Category: Usability
Gate: Run
Area: Discovery

## Assumption(s)
### A1
Statement: We believe a discovery exit that lists more than five untested claims for a first build will cause people to treat the whole list as in-scope — not as a backlog of unproven items.
Success criteria: Given a packet with 12 claims and no "in this slice" mark, 4 of 5 people say the first build includes all 12 or "the system." Given the same packet with only 3 claims marked "test before build," 4 of 5 name those 3 only.
Failure criteria: With the mark, 2 or more of 5 still take all 12 into first build. Or without the mark they already limit to a thin slice (then unbounded discovery is not what this packet is doing).

## Slice(s)
### S1 (tests A1)
Method: Two versions of one fake one-pager (not a real client system). Version A: 12 claims, no slice mark. Version B: same 12, three marked "before first build." Five people. Order mixed. Each writes what is in the first build. Count claims named.
Type: Usability
Execution: Requires manual run
Scope / time-box: 5 people. 15 minutes. Fake one-pager, disposable.

## Signal
Success signal: Unmarked: most take the whole list. Marked: ≥4 of 5 name only the three.
Failure signal: The mark does not cut scope, or the unmarked packet was already thin (wrong fixture).
Threshold: Fail → marking is not enough; change how discovery is sized. Pass → a visible slice cut works on paper. Do not promote a full discovery playbook from this.
